# Register a birth or a late registration

A birth must be registered with the civil registrar of the place where the child was born within 30 days. A birth registered after that is a delayed (late) registration and needs more documents.

> Confirm before you go: requirements and fees can change. Check with the office or the [Offices page](/government/offices).

## Births within 30 days

Hospitals and lying-in clinics usually prepare the Certificate of Live Birth and file it for you. Check before you leave that the names and dates are spelled right.

- [ ] Certificate of Live Birth (Municipal Form 102), signed by the birth attendant
- [ ] Marriage certificate of the parents, if married
- [ ] If the parents are not married and the father acknowledges the child: Affidavit of Admission of Paternity, and an Affidavit to Use the Surname of the Father (RA 9255) if the child will use his surname

## Late registration (after 30 days)

- [ ] Certificate of Live Birth (several copies; ask the office how many)
- [ ] Affidavit of Delayed Registration explaining why the birth was not registered on time
- [ ] At least two documents showing the name, date and place of birth, such as a baptismal certificate, school records, or medical or immunization records
- [ ] Negative certification from the PSA (proof that no birth record exists), if asked
- [ ] Valid IDs of the parents or the registrant

The civil registrar posts a notice of the application for 10 days before acting on it.

## Steps

1. Get the forms from the Municipal Civil Registrar in the Naic Municipal Hall.
2. Fill them out and have the affidavits notarized or sworn before the registrar.
3. Submit the documents and pay the fee at the Municipal Treasurer.
4. Claim your copy. The record reaches the PSA later; a PSA copy is usually available a few months after registration.

## Fees

Timely registration is generally free. Late registration fees follow the municipal fee ordinance.
