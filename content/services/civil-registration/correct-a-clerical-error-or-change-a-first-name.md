# Correct a clerical error or change a first name

Some mistakes in a birth, marriage or death record can be fixed at the civil registrar without going to court. RA 9048 covers clerical errors and change of first name; RA 10172 added the day and month of birth and sex, when the error is clearly clerical.

> Confirm before you go: requirements and fees can change. Check with the office or the [Offices page](/government/offices).

## What the civil registrar can fix

- Misspellings and other obvious typing errors
- Change of first name or nickname, for reasons the law allows
- Day and month of birth (not the year)
- Sex, when the record is clearly a typing error

Changes to nationality, legitimacy, surname, or year of birth need a court petition (Rule 108).

## Requirements

- [ ] Sworn petition on the civil registrar's form
- [ ] Certified copy of the record to be corrected
- [ ] At least two public or private documents showing the correct entry, such as baptismal or school records, or SSS, GSIS or voter's records
- [ ] For change of first name or sex: NBI and police clearances, and an employer's clearance or an affidavit of no employment
- [ ] For sex: medical certification from a government physician
- [ ] Change of first name, day or month of birth, or sex: proof of newspaper publication once a week for two weeks

## Steps

1. File the petition with the Municipal Civil Registrar where the record is registered. If you now live elsewhere, you can file a migrant petition with the civil registrar where you live.
2. Pay the filing fee at the Municipal Treasurer.
3. The petition is posted for 10 days, and published where required.
4. The civil registrar decides, and the PSA confirms the decision.
5. Request the annotated PSA copy once the PSA has processed it.

## Fees

RA 9048 sets the filing fee at ₱1,000 for clerical corrections and ₱3,000 for change of first name. Indigent petitioners may be exempted. Migrant petitions have an added service fee.
