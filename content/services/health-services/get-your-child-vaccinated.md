# Get your child vaccinated

Routine childhood vaccines under the Department of Health's National Immunization Program are free at the Rural Health Unit and barangay health stations.

> Confirm before you go: requirements and fees can change. Check with the office or the [Offices page](/government/offices).

## The basic schedule

The DOH schedule starts at birth and continues through the first year:

- **At birth:** BCG and hepatitis B
- **At 6, 10 and 14 weeks:** pentavalent vaccine, polio vaccine, and pneumococcal vaccine
- **At 9 and 12 months:** measles-containing vaccine

The health worker will tell you if your child needs other doses, such as inactivated polio vaccine, and will write the next date in your child's book. School-based vaccinations continue in Grade 1 and Grade 7, and HPV vaccination is offered to girls in school.

## What to bring

- [ ] Your child's Mother and Child Book or immunization card
- [ ] Your child's birth certificate or the hospital record, for the first visit

## Steps

1. Go to the Rural Health Unit or your barangay health station on its immunization day. Ask the health worker for the schedule.
2. The health worker checks your child's record and health.
3. Your child gets the vaccines due that day.
4. Keep the card safe and come back on the next date written on it.

Missed a dose? Go anyway. The health worker will help your child catch up.

## Fees

Vaccines in the National Immunization Program are free.
