# Renew your business permit

Business permits expire at the end of the year. Under the Local Government Code, the renewal and the local business tax are due within the first 20 days of January, and late payment adds a surcharge and interest.

> Confirm before you go: requirements and fees can change. Check with the office or the [Offices page](/government/offices).

## Who should renew

Every business with a current Naic business permit that will keep operating in the new year.

## Requirements

- [ ] Renewal application form
- [ ] Previous year's business permit and official receipts
- [ ] Barangay business clearance for the new year
- [ ] Sworn declaration of gross sales or receipts for the previous year, with supporting documents (such as the income tax return or audited financial statements)
- [ ] Community tax certificate (cedula) for the new year
- [ ] Other permits that expire yearly, such as the sanitary permit and fire safety inspection certificate
- [ ] Valid ID, and an authorization letter if a representative files

## Steps

1. Renew your barangay business clearance.
2. File the renewal at the BPLO. Many LGUs run a Business One-Stop Shop (BOSS) in January where the offices sit together; ask if Naic is running one.
3. Wait for the assessment of your business tax and fees.
4. Pay at the Municipal Treasurer.
5. Claim the renewed permit.

## Fees and penalties

Business taxes depend on your gross sales or receipts for the previous year. Payment after January 20 adds a surcharge of up to 25% plus monthly interest under the Local Government Code. Exact rates follow the municipal revenue code.
