# Apply for a new business permit

Every business operating in Naic needs a Mayor's permit from the Business Permits and Licensing Office (BPLO). Register your business name with the right national agency first, then get your barangay clearance, then file with the BPLO.

> Confirm before you go: requirements and fees can change. Check with the office or the [Offices page](/government/offices).

## Before the BPLO

1. **Register the business name**
   - Sole proprietorship: [DTI Business Name Registration](https://bnrs.dti.gov.ph)
   - Partnership or corporation: [SEC](https://www.sec.gov.ph)
   - Cooperative: [CDA](https://cda.gov.ph)
2. **Get a barangay business clearance** from the barangay where the business is located.

## Requirements at the BPLO

- [ ] Unified Business Application Form
- [ ] DTI, SEC or CDA registration
- [ ] Barangay business clearance
- [ ] Proof of the business location: title or tax declaration if you own it, or a contract of lease
- [ ] Community tax certificate (cedula)
- [ ] Locational or zoning clearance, if the BPLO asks for it
- [ ] Occupancy permit of the building, if applicable
- [ ] Sanitary permit, for food and similar businesses
- [ ] Valid ID of the owner or an authorized representative

The Bureau of Fire Protection inspects the place and issues a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate as part of the process.

## Steps

1. File the application and documents at the BPLO in the Naic Municipal Hall.
2. Wait for the assessment of fees and taxes.
3. Pay at the Municipal Treasurer.
4. Claim your Mayor's permit and display it where customers can see it.
5. Register with the [BIR](https://www.bir.gov.ph) before you start operating, and with SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG if you will hire workers.

## Fees and processing time

Fees and local business taxes follow the municipal revenue code and depend on the type of business and capital. The Ease of Doing Business Act (RA 11032) sets maximum processing times for government transactions.
