# Prepare for floods and typhoons

Naic sits on Manila Bay, and much of it is low-lying. Heavy monsoon rain, typhoons and storm surge are the hazards most households will face, mostly from June to November.

> Confirm before you go: requirements and fees can change. Check with the office or the [Offices page](/government/offices).

## Check your risk now

- Look up your exact address on [HazardHunterPH](https://hazardhunter.georisk.gov.ph) to see flood, storm surge and other hazards.
- Ask your barangay where your evacuation center is and how warnings are sent.

## Understand the warnings

- **Tropical cyclone wind signals (1 to 5):** issued by [PAGASA](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph). The higher the signal, the stronger the expected winds.
- **Rainfall warnings (yellow, orange, red):** red means serious flooding is expected in low areas. Be ready to evacuate.
- **Storm surge warnings:** coastal barangays should move inland when one is raised.

## Before

1. Pack a go-bag: water, ready-to-eat food for 3 days, flashlight, radio, medicines, copies of IDs in a waterproof bag, power bank, and cash.
2. Agree on a meeting place and an out-of-town contact person with your family.
3. Clear your gutters and canals, and move valuables to higher places.
4. Charge your phones and follow official advisories.

## During

1. Evacuate early when told to. Do not wait for the water to rise.
2. Never walk, swim or drive through floodwater.
3. Turn off the main power switch before water reaches outlets.
4. Keep the roads clear for rescuers. Call 911 in an emergency.

## After

1. Go home only when authorities say it is safe.
2. Treat floodwater as dirty. Boil or disinfect drinking water.
3. If you waded in floodwater, watch for fever and muscle pain, signs of leptospirosis, and see a doctor early.
4. Report damage and missing persons to your barangay.
