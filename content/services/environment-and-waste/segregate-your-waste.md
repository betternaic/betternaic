# Segregate your waste

The Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (RA 9003) requires every household to separate its garbage at home. Segregated waste is easier to collect, compost and recycle, and keeps our canals and Manila Bay cleaner.

> Confirm before you go: requirements and fees can change. Check with the office or the [Offices page](/government/offices).

## The four kinds of waste

| Type | Examples | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biodegradable | Food scraps, peelings, leaves | Compost at home, or set out on biodegradable collection days |
| Recyclable | Clean paper, cardboard, bottles, cans, hard plastics | Sell to a junk shop or bring to the barangay materials recovery facility |
| Residual | Used tissue, diapers, sachets, styrofoam | Bag securely for collection |
| Special or hazardous | Batteries, light bulbs, paint, chemicals, old electronics | Keep apart and ask your barangay or MENRO how to dispose of them |

## Rules to remember

- Burning garbage is not allowed under RA 9003 and the Clean Air Act.
- Dumping in canals, rivers, vacant lots or the sea is not allowed.
- Your barangay may refuse to collect garbage that is not segregated.

## Steps

1. Keep separate containers or sacks at home for each type.
2. Ask your barangay hall for the collection schedule in your area.
3. Put out only the type being collected that day.
